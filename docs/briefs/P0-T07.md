# P0-T07 Spike D: Smart Turn v3 in the browser — brief

Owner: main (architect-direct). Depends: P0-T04. ADRs: 01, 06, 09, 20.
Surface facts: `docs/SURFACE.md`, "Smart Turn v3" and "Smart Turn v3 input surface", both 2026-09-08.

## The question

Spike A measured 947 ms from end of speech to first audio on WebGPU, and **512 ms of it was the
VAD hangover** — waiting to be sure the person had stopped talking. That is 54% of the good
configuration's latency spent on silence, and it is the largest single cost in the pipeline. It is
also the only part that is a guess rather than work: the pipeline itself is 435 ms, inside ADR-20's
500 ms budget.

Smart Turn v3 is a semantic endpointer — it looks at the audio and predicts whether the *sentence*
is finished, rather than counting silence. If it can run in a browser and answer within roughly
150 ms of a short silence, first audio comes down to about 600 ms with no other change. That is the
difference between a demo and a conversation, which is why Spike A's write-up promoted this task
ahead of B and C.

Two ways this fails, and both are useful answers:

- **It does not run.** onnxruntime-web cannot load the graph, or the Whisper feature extraction is
  too slow in JS to be worth it. Then P1-T07 ships adaptive silence and this brief has to say what
  that design is.
- **It runs but is not better.** Feature extraction plus inference costs more than the silence it
  saves, or it fires mid-sentence often enough to be worse than waiting. Same outcome: adaptive
  silence, with numbers behind the decision.

## What is already known, and must not be re-derived

`docs/SURFACE.md` records, verified on 2026-09-08 by reading the ONNX graphs and pipecat's
`inference.py` rather than from memory:

- `input_features` float32 `[batch, 80, 800]` → `logits` float32 `[batch, 1]`, both builds.
- `logits` is already sigmoid-activated despite the name. Assert it lands in `[0, 1]` anyway.
- `-cpu.onnx` is int8 (8.68 MB), `-gpu.onnx` is fp32 (32.41 MB). **Spike A found int8 on WebGPU
  produces fluent nonsense**, so that pairing is the one to distrust, not the one to default to.
- The audio is the **last** 8 s, zero-padded **at the front** when shorter, then zero-mean
  unit-variance normalised over the whole 128 000 samples, then Whisper log-mel with
  `chunk_length: 8`. transformers.js pads at the *end* and has no `do_normalize`, so both of those
  are our job.

Read that section before writing a line of this. Getting the preprocessing subtly wrong is the
failure mode: the model will return confident, varying, wrong probabilities, and nothing in the
harness would look broken.

## How inference is triggered

This is the design the spike is really testing, so it is specified rather than left to the code.

The VAD runs as it does today, but with a **short** candidate silence — 200 ms by default,
adjustable on the page. Every time the silence crosses that threshold, the utterance so far (plus
pre-roll) goes to Smart Turn. If the probability is above the threshold, the turn ends *then*; if
not, the VAD keeps listening, and a later candidate gets another go. The 500 ms hangover stays as
the backstop, so a turn always ends even if the model never fires.

That means a turn ends in one of two ways, and the page must show which:

- **`smart-turn`** — the model called it, at `candidateMs` + feature time + inference time after
  speech stopped.
- **`hangover`** — the model never crossed the threshold and the old timer closed it, at 512 ms.

The ratio of the two, over labelled utterances, is most of the result.

## Measuring accuracy honestly

Latency is easy and meaningless on its own: a model that always says "finished" is instant and
useless. So each utterance is **labelled before it is spoken**, with a control on the page:

- **Complete** — a finished sentence. "What's the weather like today?"
- **Incomplete** — a real mid-sentence pause, the thing a silence timer gets wrong. "I was thinking
  that maybe we could…" then stop and wait.

The harness records the label with the turn and builds a confusion matrix. What matters:

- **False fire on an incomplete utterance** is the expensive error — the assistant interrupts. This
  is the number the write-up leads with.
- A miss on a complete utterance costs the 512 ms hangover, which is what happens today anyway. It
  is a disappointment, not a regression.

Ten of each label per configuration, minimum. Fewer than that is an anecdote.

## What to measure

Per candidate, from the one monotonic clock Spike A established:

| Mark | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `speechEnd` | last frame that was speech — same definition as Spike A, before any silence |
| `candidateAt` | the short silence expired; the window was cut |
| `featuresDone` | log-mel computed |
| `inferenceDone` | probability back from the model |

Report **`speechEnd → inferenceDone`** as the headline — that is the honest replacement for the
512 ms hangover, and it includes the candidate silence, which is a cost and not free. Break out
feature time and inference time separately: if features dominate, the fix is a different feature
path, not a different model, and the write-up should say so.

Also record: model load time, cold and warm; the backend actually in use, not the one requested;
the probability for every candidate, not just the firing one; and the number of candidates per
turn, because a turn that takes four inferences costs four inferences.

### Two failure modes the harness has to be able to see

1. **Constant output.** Spike A's broken q8 path was caught because different audio produced an
   identical string. The same shape of bug here would produce a plausible probability that barely
   moves. Show the spread of probabilities across turns; near-zero variance is a red flag to be
   chased before any number is believed, not a result.
2. **A wrong mel.** The worker asserts the feature block is `[1, 80, 800]` and that
   `max - min === 2.0` to a small tolerance — a property of Whisper's `(max(x, max-8) + 4) / 4`
   clamp that a misconfigured extractor will not satisfy. On failure it reports an error rather
   than a probability.

## Tests

Vitest, on the parts where an error would silently corrupt every number:

`turn-audio.test.ts`
1. Audio longer than 8 s keeps the **last** 128 000 samples, not the first.
2. Audio shorter than 8 s is padded **at the front**: the real samples end at the last index.
3. Exactly 8 s passes through unchanged in length.
4. Normalisation gives mean ≈ 0 and population variance ≈ 1 over the whole padded array, including
   the zeros — matching `do_normalize`'s all-ones attention mask rather than normalising the speech
   alone.
5. Silence in, no `NaN` out: the `+ 1e-7` under the square root is load-bearing.

`turn-metrics.test.ts`
6. Detection latency is `inferenceDone - speechEnd`, and a candidate missing a mark reports
   incomplete rather than a number, as Spike A's model does.
7. The confusion matrix counts a fire on an incomplete utterance as a false positive and a hangover
   close on a complete one as a false negative, and the rates are over the right denominators.
8. Median and worst over candidates, even counts included.

The mel itself is transformers.js's own code and is not re-tested here; it is asserted at runtime
instead, which is the check that actually runs on the machine taking the measurement.

## Notes for whoever reads this later

- Headphones. Spike A spent an evening discovering the microphone was transcribing the speakers.
- The threshold is a product decision, not a constant: make it adjustable on the page and report the
  probabilities, so the write-up can say what a different threshold would have done rather than
  needing another run.
- If the answer is "runs fine, but the features cost 200 ms in JS", that is a real finding and points
  at a Rust companion path (ADR-04) rather than at giving up. Say so; do not round it to a no.
