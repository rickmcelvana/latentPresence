# P1-T07 — VAD and turn detection

- **lane:** main
- **status:** landed 2026-09-13, gate green at 545 TypeScript / 17 Rust
- **written:** 2026-09-13, as a handoff. Everything below the outcome is the starting
  position it was, kept so the arguments with it stay legible.

## Outcome

**The brief's plan held; the done-when's split was taken as proposed.** Logic in
`packages/core/src/turn` tested on scripted probabilities; two thin workers plus the model
code they share in `packages/ml-web/src/turn`; drivers and `speculativeTranscription` in
`packages/providers/src/turn`; `pnpm live:turn` for real speech. `/spike/turn` is gone.

**Where it argued back:**

- **Recognition is cancelled when speech resumes, not when the judge says "not finished."**
  A low probability does not change the audio; if the hangover then closes the turn, the
  transcript in flight is of exactly the right words.
- **Silero's reference feeds 576 samples, not 512** — 64 of context. The spikes fed 512.
  Measured both: context detects onset up to 64 ms earlier and stops the bare frame's early
  speech-end in noise (up to 107 ms). Promoted code follows the reference.
- **The caveat about Silero on synthetic speech did not bite.** No microphone fallback.
- **Smart Turn ends sentences at inner pauses whose prefix sounds finished**, and the
  faster the judge the more it does it. **ADR-25 (proposed)** makes a model end retractable
  until the hangover would have fired. Before it, 4 of 6 complete sentences were cut off at
  WebGPU latency; after, 0 of 12, median 168 ms, worst 220 ms.
- **`STTProvider.transcribe` is lazy**, so the obvious `recognise` wiring silently loses the
  overlap. `speculativeTranscription` exists for that, with a test that fails if it regresses.
- **P1-T06's Moonshine budget conclusion was wrong** for one-to-two-second speech: the
  library's `floor(s) * 6` truncated 9 of 15 candidate windows. `asr.worker.ts` now sends
  `ceil(s * 6)`; 0% WER on every kept window.

**Still open:** the WebGPU latency on this onnxruntime version and how often a human
triggers retraction — both need the microphone in the app (`docs/TASKS.md`).

## Why this is `main` and not `sub`

Spike D measured this on real hardware and its numbers are the contract. But the spike
measured *a configuration*, and P1-T07 has to decide what happens when reality misses it —
a late answer, a probability that never crosses, a machine with no WebGPU. Those are
judgement calls against ADR-20's budget, and the budget is the product. The mechanical
half (promoting two workers) is small; the decisions are the task.

## What already exists, and where it goes

This is the third time this shape has come up, and it should be done the same way P1-T05
and P1-T06 did it: **the spike already contains the working reference; promote it, do not
rewrite it.**

Follow `packages/ml-web/src/asr/` for the shape: a `messages.ts` carrying the worker
contract, model catalog and **measured** download sizes, a worker, a `create-worker.ts`, a
structural `WorkerPort` so the consumer tests in node against a four-line fake.

The endpointing *logic* — candidate windows, the hangover backstop, the overlap with
recognition — does not belong in `ml-web`. It is conversation logic with no model in it,
so it belongs in `packages/core`, beside the chunker, and it should be testable with a
scripted stream of probabilities and no model at all.

## The numbers that are already decided

From `docs/spikes/D-smart-turn.md` (2026-09-08) and ADR-21:

- **fp32 on WebGPU, 100 ms candidate silence, 0.7 threshold.** 168 ms.
- **int8 is the no-GPU fallback and must never be offered on WebGPU** — it does not load
  there at all. Same shape as P1-T06's rule, and it should be enforced the same way: in the
  provider constructor *and* in the worker's load, so no caller reaches it by forgetting.
- **The 500 ms hangover stays as the backstop.** An answer arriving after it is reported
  **late**, not missed. Spike D saw two.
- The separation is wide — finished sentences 0.70–0.99, trailed-off ones 0.007–0.023 — so
  the exact threshold matters much less than it looks. Do not spend the session tuning it.

## The done-when needs a decision before it can be met

> *tests with recorded audio fixtures detect turn ends within 300 ms of the labelled point*

**There are no recorded audio fixtures and this repo should not grow any.** Committing
audio means committing binaries, and ADR-09's spirit is that assets do not live here.

The suggestion, from what P1-T06 learned: **split it.**

- **In the gate**, test the *logic* against a scripted probability stream and a fake worker
  port — candidate windows opening and closing, the hangover firing, a late answer being
  labelled late, the overlap handing the same window to recognition and to the endpointer
  at the same moment. None of that needs audio, and all of it is where the bugs will be.
- **In a live check** (`pnpm live:turn`), use the closed loop P1-T06 built: **Kokoro
  synthesises the utterances and the labelled point is where the synthesis ended**, so the
  label is exact rather than hand-marked. Silence of a chosen length is trivial to
  concatenate. This tests the models on real speech without a fixture or a microphone.

A caveat to check before relying on it: **Silero VAD may not fire on synthesised speech the
way it does on a microphone** — Kokoro's output has no room tone, no breath and no
handling noise. If it does not, say so in `docs/SURFACE.md` and fall back to a live check
Rick runs with a microphone, rather than quietly loosening the test.

## The done-when's second half is the load-bearing one

> *the candidate window is handed to recognition (P1-T06) at the same moment it is handed
> to the endpointer — ADR-21's overlap, without which the pipeline is 40 ms over budget*

P1-T06 built for exactly this: `BrowserSTTProvider.transcribe` takes an
`AsyncIterable<AudioChunk>` and **a cancelled call yields nothing at all**, which is what
makes speculative recognition safe to start. The discarded case is the common one.

So the overlap is: open a candidate window → hand it to *both* → if the endpointer says the
turn did not end, cancel the recognition and drop it. If that works, the recognition for a
turn that *did* end is already in flight when the endpointer confirms it.

## Watch for

- **Sample rates.** Silero and Smart Turn want 16 kHz, same as recognition. `resample()` in
  `packages/ml-web/src/asr/resample.ts` already exists and is tested; use it rather than a
  second copy. Resample **once** and hand the same buffer to both consumers — doing it
  twice is both wasteful and a place for the two paths to disagree.
- **The 40 ms.** ADR-21's overlap is worth 40 ms against a 500 ms budget. If the
  implementation makes the overlap awkward, that is a real trade to put to Rick, not
  something to silently drop.
- **Nothing here has been measured on this transformers.js/onnxruntime version.** Spike D's
  168 ms is from 2026-09-08. Re-measuring is cheap and belongs in the live check.
- **`/spike/turn` goes when this lands** — `PROJECT.md` records that trigger explicitly, and
  `apps/web/vite.config.ts`'s guard has entries that come out with it.
