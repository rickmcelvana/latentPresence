## Measured from the recordings

No ffmpeg or OpenCV on the box, so the Browser pane did both halves: `decodeAudioData` on
the webm for the audio, and a `<video>` seeked every 1/30 s into a canvas for the picture.
**Mouth openness** is the count of lip-coloured pixels (red − green > 45) in a 120×80 box
around the mouth — generous enough to absorb the life layer's head motion; closed reads
~2, wide open ~1500. **The audio** is RMS in 20 ms windows. **Lag** is the shift of the
audio that best correlates with the mouth; positive means the mouth is late.

| segment | frames | best lag | within 90% of the peak | r |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kokoro, file 1 | 283 | **−20 ms** | — | 0.41 |
| Kokoro, file 2 | 261 | **+10 ms** | −20 … +20 ms | 0.37 |
| Rick's audio, file 2 | 919 | +110 ms | +60 … +130 ms | 0.40 |

**On Kokoro — the voice the product speaks with — the mouth is within one video frame
(33 ms at 30 fps) of the sound, either side.** That is the done-when (< 80 ms) with margin,
and it agrees with the offline measurement of the chain (+35 ms best fit, `docs/SURFACE.md`)
once the recording's own path is allowed for.

**Rick's audio reads +110 ms, and it is not the mouth closing late.** Per pause, the mouth
shut a median ~50 ms after the audio stopped (13 pauses: 20–230 ms) — no slower than on
Kokoro (4 pauses: 23–153 ms). What differs is the material: its loudness envelope has its
energy at **0.5 Hz** (slow swells) where speech has it at 3–6 Hz (syllables), with long
pauses (median 480 ms). The mouth follows the analyser's *dB* loudness, which flattens a
slow swell, so whole-signal correlation against RMS leans late on audio shaped like that.
On speech-shaped audio the two agree. If a future voice is breathy, sung or reverberant,
this is the measurement to repeat.

## What the numbers are not

- **The recording is not the room.** Its audio is taken from the playback node before the
  device, so the device's output latency (which makes the mouth look *earlier*) is absent;
  display latency (later) is absent too; and MediaRecorder's own muxing adds an offset
  nobody measured. The eye test on speakers is the one that covers those — and it passed.
- **Pixel counting is a coarse gauge** (r ≈ 0.4 against 0.75 offline): video compression,
  30 fps, and a shape metric that cannot tell `oh` from `aa`.
