## Measured from the recordings

No ffmpeg or OpenCV on the box, so the Browser pane did both halves: `decodeAudioData` on
the webm for the audio, and a `<video>` seeked every 1/30 s into a canvas for the picture.
**Mouth openness in the video** is the count of lip-coloured pixels (red − green > 45) in a
120×80 box around the mouth — closed reads ~2, wide open ~1500. The second file's "custom"
audio is **also Kokoro** (`af_heart`, generated by Rick a week earlier), which matters below.

**The method that held up splits the chain in two**, because each half correlates well:

| segment | video mouth behind the pipeline's mouth | pipeline's mouth behind loudness | **total** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kokoro fixture (file 2) | +10 ms (r 0.46) | +50 ms (r 0.72) | **~60 ms** |
| Rick's `af_heart` Kokoro (file 2) | +10 ms (r 0.59) | +40 ms (r 0.56) | **~50 ms** |

"The pipeline's mouth" is the recording's own audio run back through `tapAnalyser` +
`LipSync` offline (`OfflineAudioContext`, 60 fps) — what the live page should have drawn.
So **the live system — rAF, WebGL, capture — adds about 10 ms, and the analysis itself is
40–50 ms behind loudness**; the mouth ends up ~50–60 ms behind the sound. **Under the
80 ms done-when, with ~20–30 ms of margin, not the "within one frame" first reported.** The
loudness envelope here looks 20 ms *ahead* (RMS over [t, t+20 ms]) where P2-T04's offline
measurement looked 20 ms back, which accounts for 35 ms then against 40–50 now.

### Withdrawn the same day

The first pass compared the video's pixel count straight against loudness and reported
−20 ms, +10 ms and **+110 ms** (the last on Rick's file, explained as "audio not shaped like
speech"). **All three are withdrawn**: that comparison is two noisy signals head to head
(r 0.3–0.4, peaks 60–100 ms wide), and the explanation was wrong — Rick's file is Kokoro. A
per-onset reading (mouth 150–360 ms "late") is withdrawn too: loudness rises on a word's
first consonant while the mouth rightly waits for the vowel, so it measures phonetics.

## What the numbers are not

- **The recording is not the room.** Its audio is taken from the playback node before the
  device, so the device's output latency (which makes the mouth look *earlier*) is absent;
  display latency (later) is absent too; and MediaRecorder's own muxing adds an offset
  nobody measured. The eye test on speakers is the one that covers those — and it passed.
- **Pixel counting is a coarse gauge** (r ≈ 0.4 against 0.75 offline): video compression,
  30 fps, and a shape metric that cannot tell `oh` from `aa`.

## Outcome

P2-T04's done-when met: **D-24** — by eye, and ~50–60 ms by measurement against an 80 ms
bar. `DEFAULT_MOUTH_PARAMS` unchanged. **If the margin ever needs to grow**, the analysis
half is the one to cut: the voice's PCM is known before it plays, so visemes could be
computed from the samples at the playback position rather than from an analyser that can
only look back.
