## Reading

- **Echo cancellation holds on this box.** Row 7's answer played 16.2 s through speakers
  with the microphone live and the barge-in gate never ducked: the click report has two
  ducks in the whole session, both committed barge-ins (the simulated one and Rick's
  "wait"). Chromium's AEC removes Web Audio output from `getUserMedia` completely here, not
  merely enough to stay under 200 ms. One machine, one volume — not a general result.
- **A cough never reached the gate either.** No duck-only event anywhere, which matches
  "nothing happened": `noiseSuppression` and Silero between them did not call it speech.
  ADR-26's duck-and-recover stage is therefore built and tested, but no person has heard it.
- **The microphone path works on its first run.** Moonshine transcribed Rick's speech
  (row 5's "What was the afternoon? like," is punctuation, not words); model turn ends
  **228–253 ms** after speech on the headphone rows; a real "wait" committed a barge-in.
  **No ADR-25 retraction** in the four microphone turns (no `turn resumed` line).
- **The spoken-prefix estimate matched the ear** on the one real cut, and on the exact word
  ("because", after "anything,") that `pnpm live:bargein` had flagged as a place it can run
  one word ahead.
- **0 clicks of 58 events** by the output check, 176 s rendered, peak 0.927, 0 clamped.
- **Smart Turn scored "What was the afternoon like?" 0.01–0.02 three times** (both simulated
  asks and the speakers-on ask) and 0.98 once (headphones); the 512 ms hangover ended those
  three. Too few to act on; P1-T14 judges turn-end latency and should look at questions.
- **"frame lag: capture 154 ms" is the harness's clock, not a backlog.** It appears only after
  the switch from headphones to speakers and holds at 154–155 ms for 45 s; after a 688 ms
  stall at 188.99 s it returns to 154, not to ~0, which a real queue that could catch up
  would do. So the capture context's clock stepped against `performance.now()` when the
  output device changed, and the one-time `clockOffset` in `startCapture` went stale. The
  session itself runs on frame time only (`VoiceSession` confirms on `frame.at`), so nothing
  but the harness's cross-clock columns is affected — row 7's 1387 ms carries it, and still
  has ~250 ms unexplained. One reading; not chased.
